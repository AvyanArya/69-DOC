# Idea test: a simulated customer panel

Simulated customers are a cheap way to find **objections, segments and pricing questions**.
They are not a demand forecast: model-made customers are kinder and more alike than real
ones. Say this in the report, and always end with a real-world test.

## 1. Pin down the idea

One paragraph: what it is, who it's for, price, how it's bought (one-off, subscription),
and the closest alternatives a buyer already has. Start from `marketing/product.md` if it
exists, and ask only for what's missing.

## 2. Build the panel (100 personas)

Spread them on purpose, not all the "obvious" buyer:
- ~60% plausible target customers across 4–6 distinct segments,
- ~25% adjacent buyers (could buy for someone else, or for work),
- ~15% skeptics and people on tight budgets.

Each persona, one line in a table: name, age, situation, monthly spend in the category,
current alternative and its price, what they care about most, one concrete reason they'd say
no. Vary income, location and attitudes realistically; avoid stereotypes.

## 3. Pitch each one independently

Same short pitch for everyone. For each persona decide: **buy / maybe / pass**, the most
likely objection in their own words, and the price they'd actually pay. Judge each persona
on their budget and alternative, not on whether the idea is nice. Default to *pass* unless
the persona has a clear reason to switch.

For independence, evaluate in batches (e.g. 4 subagents × 25 personas, given only the pitch
and their personas) rather than all 100 in one running list where answers anchor.

## 4. Report

- Buy / maybe / pass counts, overall and **by segment**. The best segment is the finding.
- Top 5 objections, grouped, with a representative quote each.
- Price: share who'd buy at the asked price, at −30%, and at +30%.
- The surprise: a segment or use the user didn't expect (e.g. businesses buying for staff).
- Changes to the offer that would flip the most "maybe"s.
- Caveat in one line: simulated buyers overstate interest, so read the counts as relative,
  not absolute.

## 5. Check it with real people (the actual test)

Pick the cheapest that fits, aimed at the best segment:
- 5–10 short conversations with people in that segment ("how do you handle X today, what
  does it cost you?"; don't pitch until the end);
- a one-page landing page with the price and a pre-order or waitlist button, plus a small
  ad spend or posts where that segment gathers;
- a manual pilot: sell 10 by hand before building anything.
Set a pass mark in advance (e.g. 5% of visitors pre-order).
